Why do we choose an education, and what do we take from it? I studied English literature because I enjoyed literature and humanities. I wasn’t thinking much about future jobs or money. UX research attracted me in my first job, but many openings were design roles, so I learnt design and did research too. In practice, much of the work is getting people to agree, planning the team’s work, keeping components consistent and checking whether a flow makes sense. Even work you enjoy includes things you don’t enjoy. What gives you energy, and which less enjoyable parts are you willing to take on?

09 / SOMETHING I’M STILL THINKING ABOUT

MORE OUTPUT.
LESS THINKING?

We learnt how to use the tools.

Now, some things start
with a single prompt.

What if tools could soon
do almost everything?

“I can make more.
Am I thinking less?”What am I learning?
What thinking am I outsourcing?
This is a question I’m reflecting on, not a prediction that tools will do everything. Work that once meant learning many tool steps can sometimes start with one prompt, though the result still needs checking. Producing more is exciting, but I also catch myself relying on AI instead of working through an idea. Am I using it to explore my thinking, or letting it make the choices for me? This leads into giving AI a goal, retaining decisions and reviewing its work.
10 / OPEN TO POSSIBILITY, SCEPTICAL OF THE OUTPUT

LET AI RUN
WITH YOUR LOGIC.

  • Give it a goal and a scope
  • Decide what to delegate
  • Keep decisions within your control
  • Review and refine
“Something got done.
Do I understand it?”Why did you do it this way?
Why did this happen?
What I value in graduates: how they think about using AI, which decisions they retain, and whether they can explain and check the result. There is decision paralysis and a risk of depending so much on it that we stop thinking. Explanations need checking too.
